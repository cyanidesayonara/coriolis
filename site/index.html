Hardware & architecture

Three iterations of the same room-first idea: write scenes once against an abstract framebuffer so desktop, Teensy 4.1, and browser execute identical C++11 code.

Planned hardware bill of materials

  • Panels4× 64×64 HUB75E (1/32 scan, P4 or P3)
  • ControllerTeensy 4.1 + SmartLED Shield V5
  • Power supply5 V 40 A Mean Well LRS-200-5 + bulk caps
  • Current safetyDynamic per-frame brightness limiter (~25 A)
  • InputTSOP38438 IR on pin 16 (Apple / 17-key NEC)
  • AudioDFPlayer Mini + 40 mm speaker + microSD voice pack
  • EnclosureMagnetic M3 panel mounts + 3 mm smoked cast acrylic

Software invariants

Every visual mode renders solely into FrameBuffer through a Context& struct carrying time, held keys, palette, audio cues, and monotonic milliseconds. No scene allocates on the heap per frame, and no scene calls platform headers.

One shared shell: the boot splash, slide/crossfade transitions, categorized launcher, settings menu, night-mode brightness floor, toasts, and debounced settings persistence live in scenes/shell.h — shared line-for-line between firmware, desktop raylib simulator, and this WebAssembly page.
