Step 1

Choose the panels

Four 64 × 64 HUB75 panels in a 2 × 2 square make the 128 × 128 display. The pixel pitch sets the size; the firmware is the same for all of them.

PitchOne panelDisplayAvailability

P5320 mm640 mm64×64 P5 panels are rare (most P5 panels are 64×32). Not recommended.

Recommendation: P4 when the seller confirms the checklist below; otherwise P3.

Compatibility checklist — ask before paying

  • 64 × 64, 1/32 scan, HUB75E (address lines A–E; E on HUB75 pin 8). The SmartLED Shield V5 drives E natively.
  • Driver chips: ICN2028, FM6124, DP5220 and similar are fine. FM6126A works with one firmware option (page 7). ICN2038S, ICN2053, GS6238S and “AB” panels (only A/B address lines) do not work with SmartMatrix.
  • Ask for a photo of the back showing the chips, and order all four in one batch so colour and wiring match.
  • HackSpark's P4 listing says “1:16 scan” — ask. A true 1/16-scan 64×64 needs a different panel type and can't use C-shape stacking.
Buy the SmartLED Shield V5 first. It is discontinued at Crowd Supply, SparkFun and Adafruit; Smart Prototyping still lists it (ships from China). The design is open source: the Gerbers and BOM in the SmartMatrix repository can be fabricated and assembled (e.g. JLCPCB) as a fallback.

Step 3

Wiring

Teensy pins used by the shield

2 (OE), 3 (LAT), 6, 7 (CLK), 9–13 (panel data), and 4, 5, 22 reserved for its APA102 output. Pin 13 is panel data, so the Teensy's LED flickers — normal.

The previous hardware manual put the IR receiver on pin 2. That is the panels' OE line — use pin 16.

Power

  • Four 64 × 64 panels draw 4–5 A each at full white (16–20 A in total). The LRS-200-5 (40 A) has plenty of headroom. With the 22 A LRS-150F-5, cap brightness at compile time: -DCORIOLIS_MAX_BRIGHTNESS=180. Showing the clock, Coriolis uses a few amps.
  • Run each panel's power pigtail straight to the PSU terminals, or one 1.5 mm² pair per two panels (2.5 mm² for a single trunk). Ferrule every end. Fit a 1000–2200 µF capacitor across each pair at the panels.
  • The shield feeds the Teensy from its JST input through a 0.5 A diode. Cut the VUSB–VIN pad bridge on the underside of the Teensy so USB and the 5 V rail never fight. Afterwards the Teensy only runs with the PSU on — flash it with the PSU on.
  • Power the DFPlayer (and later the ESP32) from the 5 V rail, not through the Teensy.
  • The TSOP must run from 3.3 V: Teensy 4.1 pins are not 5 V tolerant.
Mains safety. Earth the PSU's ⏚ terminal and any metal frame. Keep the mains terminals covered. Use the fused, switched IEC inlet and strain-relieve the mains lead. If you are not comfortable wiring mains, have an electrician do this part.
Step 4

Assembly

  1. Teensy. Solder headers if needed. Cut the VUSB–VIN pads. Solder the CR2032 holder to VBAT and GND.
  2. Shield. Plug the Teensy into the shield (the shield covers the 4.0 footprint; snap off its perforated tab to reach pins 24–41 if ever needed). Fit the JST power pigtail.
  3. IR receiver. TSOP pins, lens facing you: OUT · GND · Vs. Vs → 100 Ω → 3.3 V, 10 µF from Vs to GND at the sensor, OUT → pin 16. Mount it behind a small hole at the frame edge.
  4. DFPlayer. VCC → 5 V rail, GND → common ground, TX → pin 0, RX ← 1 kΩ ← pin 1, SPK1/SPK2 → speaker (neither side to ground).
  5. Bench-test with one panel before building the frame (Step 6).
  6. Frame. For P4: 2020 rails sized so the 512 mm panel square sits inside with ~2 mm play (≈ 550 mm outside), plus 2–3 cross-members behind the panel seams carrying steel strip for the magnetic mounts. Butt the panels seam-to-seam before tightening. A wooden box: 70–90 mm deep sides, MDF or ACM back with steel strips, French cleat (≈ 5–6 kg total).
  7. Chain the panels (Step 5). Mount the electronics plate on a cross-member and the PSU low in the frame with space around it. Cut ventilation slots top and bottom.
  8. Diffuser. Smoked acrylic directly on the panels gives crisp, high-contrast pixels; a grid louver (walls ≤ 1 mm at P4) under a tinted sheet gives square pixels.

Step 5

Panel layout

The firmware drives one chain of four panels as 128 × 128. How they're chained is set by CORIOLIS_PANEL_OPTIONS at the top of firmware/coriolis/coriolis.ino.

Data order within a row depends on the panels' IN/OUT direction — so don't guess: use the test pattern. Over USB serial (115 200 baud) type t: four numbered, coloured quadrants appear with an arrow in the top-left corner. When 1-2 / 3-4 read in order and the arrow points at the top-left, the layout is right. If the whole picture is merely rotated, use Settings → Rotate instead. SM_HUB75_OPTIONS_BOTTOM_TO_TOP_STACKING starts the chain from the bottom row; combine options with |.

Step 6

Firmware and bring-up

Toolchain (Windows)

winget install ArduinoSA.CLI
powershell -File firmware/setup.ps1     # Teensy core, SmartMatrix 4 + patch, shared code
arduino-cli compile --fqbn teensy:avr:teensy41 firmware/coriolis
arduino-cli upload  --fqbn teensy:avr:teensy41 firmware/coriolis

If your Windows user name has non-ASCII letters (e.g. “Nykänen”), GCC can't find its own headers: map the folders to a drive letter first (subst X: C:\path) and build from there. Options such as CORIOLIS_PANEL_OPTIONS and CORIOLIS_MAX_BRIGHTNESS are defines at the top of the sketch.

Bring-up, one stage at a time

  1. Teensy alone over USB (before cutting VUSB–VIN, or with the PSU on). Flash, open a serial monitor at 115 200 baud, type i: it prints the time and the active scene.
  2. One panel as panel 1, powered. Type t: red quadrant 1 appears. Blank or noise → wrong panel type, or FM6126A chips: set SM_HUB75_OPTIONS_FM6126A_RESET_AT_START.
  3. All four panels. Test pattern again; fix the chain option (Step 5). t again leaves it.
  4. Remote. Each button prints [IR] 0x........ -> n. n = 0 means the code isn't mapped yet — add it in RemoteMap::decode().
  5. Sound. Generate the card (python tools/generate_voice_pack.py), copy 01, 02 and ADVERT to a FAT32 microSD, insert it in the DFPlayer. Start a Yoga session: countdown ticks, then the pose name.
  6. Time. The Teensy Loader sets the RTC to your PC's local time on upload; the first boot of each new build converts it to UTC (the RTC keeps UTC and the display applies the zone and EU summer time). Check it; fix it in Settings → Clock, or send T<unix seconds UTC> over serial — in PowerShell: "T$([DateTimeOffset]::UtcNow.ToUnixTimeSeconds())".

Troubleshooting

SymptomLikely cause / fix
Panels dark, Teensy LED flickersNo panel power; ribbon on a panel's OUT side; FM6126A panels (Step 6).
Random pixels / garbage rows1/16 vs 1/32 scan mismatch; incompatible driver chips; long or poor ribbons.
Quadrants in the wrong placeChain option (Step 5). Whole picture rotated → Settings → Rotate.
Red and blue swappedSome panels are RBG/BGR: swap the channels in blit().
Clock 1–2 h off after flashingCheck Zone / Summer in Settings → Clock, then the hour.
Clock lost after power cutCR2032 missing or flat on VBAT.
Remote ignoredReceiver on 3.3 V and pin 16? Watch for [IR] lines; map unknown codes.
No soundFAT32 card with 01, 02, ADVERT; volume in Settings; 1 kΩ on DFPlayer RX.
